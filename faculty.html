Faculty Mentors

DPI identified three faculty mentors through a structured call across UIUC, UIC, and UIS, selecting researchers with active One Health-aligned projects, existing Illinois community partnerships, and availability to guide a graduate scholar through the 2026 cohort year.

2026 One Health Projects
Animal Health
Leptospirosis in Cook County Wildlife

Investigating leptospirosis transmission in skunks and raccoons across Cook County, using wildlife pathology, molecular testing, and spatial analysis to identify disease hotspots and inform prevention strategies.

Human Health
Maternal & Infant Health Risk Index

Building a county-level predictive risk index for preterm birth and infant mortality in Illinois, integrating social vulnerability, economic conditions, prenatal care access, and environmental exposures into a single actionable score for local health departments.

Environmental Health
Prairie Walking Path, Markham, IL

Evaluating a community-led native greenspace initiative at the Markham Public Library, using participatory science and birdwatching programs to track biodiversity, measure environmental health improvements, and foster community connection in an underserved South Suburban community.

Mentorship Begins, Feb 2026 Each of the three faculty mentors is matched with a graduate scholar. Scholars are oriented to their project scope, introduced to community partners, and begin structured weekly check-ins with their mentor.
Field Research, Mar 2026 Scholars move into active data collection and community engagement. Projects span Cook County wildlife surveillance, county-level maternal health modeling, and greenspace biodiversity assessment in Markham, IL.
Course Modules Delivered, Apr 2026 Faculty submit four completed One Health training modules to DPI. Each module is reviewed for accuracy, accessibility, and alignment with Illinois public health workforce needs before final production.
Stakeholder Review, Summer 2026 DPI and IDPH host a One Health Stakeholder Course Feedback event. Public health practitioners, local health department staff, and community partners review module content and provide structured feedback before statewide rollout.
Scholars Placed, 2027 Graduate scholars complete the program and enter the Illinois public health workforce as trained One Health coordinators, carrying applied research experience and cross-sector relationships built during the 2026 cohort year.
