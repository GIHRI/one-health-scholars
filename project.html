01

Program Overview

The Illinois One Health Scholars Program is a unique workforce development initiative designed to prepare future leaders to address complex challenges at the intersection of human, animal, and environmental health.

Through research mentorship, applied public health experience, and interdisciplinary collaboration, scholars gain the knowledge, skills, and practical experience needed to advance One Health solutions across Illinois.

The program bridges academic research and public health practice by connecting students, researchers, and Local Health Departments in a shared effort to strengthen health systems and improve community well-being.

02

Why One Health?

The health of people, animals, and the environment is deeply interconnected. Emerging infectious diseases, climate change, environmental degradation, food systems, water quality, and community health inequities do not occur in isolation.

Addressing these challenges requires professionals who can work across disciplines, integrate diverse perspectives, and develop solutions that recognize these connections. The One Health Scholars Program was created to help build this next generation of leaders.

Human Health
Population health, equity, and community well-being.
Animal Health
Surveillance, zoonotic disease, and shared ecosystems.
Environmental Health
Climate, water, food systems, and the natural world.
03

The Scholar Journey & Experience

The program follows a deliberate four-stage path that moves each scholar from selection, through guided research, into applied public health practice, and ultimately into leadership. At every stage, scholars build real, transferable capability.

1Select

A small cohort is selected each year through a competitive process.

Selected for
  • Academic achievement
  • Leadership potential
  • Community-health commitment
2Discover

Three months on a mentored One Health research project.

Research experience
  • Faculty mentorship
  • Data analysis and inquiry
  • Research communication
3Apply

A one-year internship with a Local Health Department.

Public health experience
  • Health department placement
  • Applied public health practice
  • Community outreach
4Lead

Graduates lead across disciplines, organizations, and sectors.

Professional growth
  • Leadership development
  • Scientific communication
  • Interdisciplinary collaboration

Cohort Selected, January 2026 A small cohort of graduate scholars is chosen through a competitive application process, selected for academic excellence, leadership potential, and a drive to improve community health across Illinois.
Mentorship Begins, February 2026 Each of the three faculty mentors is matched with a graduate scholar. Scholars are oriented to their project scope, introduced to community partners, and begin structured weekly check-ins with their mentor.
Field Research, March 2026 Scholars move into active data collection and community engagement. Projects span Cook County wildlife surveillance, county-level maternal health modeling, and greenspace biodiversity assessment in Markham, IL.
Course Modules Delivered, April 2026 Faculty submit four completed One Health training modules to DPI. Each module is reviewed for accuracy, accessibility, and alignment with Illinois public health workforce needs before final production.
Stakeholder Review, Summer 2026 DPI and IDPH host a One Health Stakeholder Course Feedback event. Public health practitioners, local health department staff, and community partners review module content and provide structured feedback before statewide rollout.
Scholars Placed, 2027 Graduate scholars complete the program and enter the Illinois public health workforce as trained One Health coordinators, carrying applied research experience and cross-sector relationships built during the 2026 cohort year.
05

Building Capacity & Program Impact

The program's impact extends well beyond the scholars themselves. It serves as a bridge between education, research, and public service: participating researchers and faculty collaborate with the program team to develop One Health educational resources, training modules, and implementation tools that support Local Health Departments across Illinois. The result is a dual investment.

Investment One
Tomorrow's Professionals

Developing future One Health professionals: scholars trained through research and a year of real field practice.

&
Investment Two
Today's Workforce

Strengthening the capacity of today's public health workforce through faculty-built One Health training delivered statewide.

A pathway for emerging leaders to transform knowledge into action, connecting research, practice, and community engagement to advance the health of people, animals, and the environment.
